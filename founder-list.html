A founder list filtered by your thesis

Why deal flow is noisy

Most inbound pitches miss on something basic: the round is too large or too small for your ticket, the stage is wrong, the sector isn't in your thesis, or the company is in a market you don't cover. Each one still costs time to read, decline and track — and the startups that do fit often arrive late, through whoever happened to forward them.

A founder list fixes this only if it records the facts that decide fit, and if your sourcing filters on them before a pitch reaches you.

Where good deal flow comes from

  • Founders you've backed. They know your thesis and their peers — usually the best-qualified referrals you'll get.
  • Co-investors and scouts. Shared deals from people whose judgement you trust.
  • Accelerators and demo days. Concentrated access to companies at a known stage.
  • Sector communities and events. Where founders in your thesis already gather.
  • Inbound and databases. Broad but noisy; useful when filtered hard on stage, ticket and geography.

Qualifying founders fast

Decide on fit before you read a deck. Three checks remove most mismatches:

  1. Ticket and stage — does their round, at their stage, accommodate the cheque you write?
  2. Thesis — does the company sit inside your sectors, or is it adjacent?
  3. Geography — can you invest in their market and structure?

Only then look at traction and team. A short first call — twenty minutes — is enough to decide whether a deeper look is worth it.

Publish your thesis. A clear, public statement of your ticket, stages, sectors and geography is the cheapest filter there is. Founders who read it self-select.

Questions investors ask

Where do angel investors and VCs find startups?

Mostly through their network — founders they've backed, co-investors and scouts — plus accelerators, sector communities, inbound and databases. Referrals from trusted founders tend to be the best qualified.

What should a founder list include?

For each startup: founders, stage, round size and amount committed, sector, geography, traction such as ARR and growth, the instrument, and how the company reached you.

How can investors reduce unsolicited pitches?

Publish a clear thesis and use channels that filter on it. On Binder, founders outside your stated ticket and thesis never reach you, and a connection requires both sides to say yes.
